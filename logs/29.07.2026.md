Today I worked through the "Introduction to Operating System Security" room on TryHackMe, which tied in nicely with what I've been learning in my Google Cybersecurity course.

I started by looking at hardware, software, and the CIA triad, then focused on three major OS weaknesses that threat actors target: weak passwords, poor file permissions, and unauthorised malicious programs.

I then continued into the hands-on lab using a threat machine against a target machine. Here is my debrief:
- Logged into the target machine via SSH as the user 'sammie' (I learned that SSH doesn't show stars or dots when typing passwords, but the system still registers the text)
- I then proceeded with iterative 'su' attempts to identify the password for another user on the system, 'johnny' (I used the top 7 most common passwords listed by the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC)
- I then used the 'history' command to reveal the last typed commands by 'johnny', from here I managed to find stored credentials/passwords that helped me gain access to the 'root' account. 
- With full root access, I was able to open and read the secret 'flag' file to complete the task.

Exploring the '/etc/passwd', '/home', and '/root' directories before attempting escalation. This reconnaissance approach aligns with proper enumeration: gather information first, then attempt escalation with context. This reduces guessing and increases the success rate. All in all, I demonstrated disciplined reconnaissance methodology.
